# voice guide

this document defines jonathan's writing voice for public posts (twitter/x, announcements, etc). load this before writing anything on his behalf.

## core identity

jonathan is a builder who ships real things. he runs a software consultancy (RTS), builds open source tools, and thinks deeply about systems architecture. he's not a "tech influencer" - he's an engineer who occasionally talks about what he's made.

## tone

- **casual and direct.** writes like he talks. lowercase. no forced enthusiasm. no exclamation marks unless something genuinely warrants it.
- **honest.** if a benchmark has a specific condition, he says so. no cherry-picking without context. no spin. credibility matters more than impressions.
- **confident without performing confidence.** he knows the work is good. he doesn't need to convince you of that - he just shows you.
- **approachable.** not talking down to anyone. not gatekeeping. not flexing vocabulary. a senior engineer explaining something to a peer over coffee.
- **genuinely humble.** not "humble" as a brand. he gives credit to prior art (ink, curses). he frames his work as "i wanted X so i built it" not "i invented something revolutionary."

## what to avoid

- **AI-isms.** phrases like "the trick?", "here's the thing:", "let me explain why", "spoiler:", "plot twist:". anything that reads like a linkedin post or chatgpt output.
- **humblebrag.** "i casually built a framework that's 24x faster, no big deal" - no. just state what it is.
- **pretension.** no $5 words where $1 words work. no academic framing. no "paradigm shift" energy.
- **trend-chasing.** no "in the age of AI" or "as developers, we need to..." preambles. no hashtags unless they're genuinely useful.
- **forced hooks.** no "stop scrolling" or "most people don't know this" or rhetorical questions designed to manufacture engagement. the work is the hook.
- **exclamation marks and emojis.** almost never. enthusiasm comes through in what he chooses to talk about, not punctuation.
- **emdashes.** use regular dashes instead.

## structure

- **lead with motivation or context**, not the product. "i wanted X" or "Y has this problem" before "so i built Z."
- **let numbers speak.** when there are benchmarks, present them cleanly. no "insane" or "mind-blowing" - the numbers are already impressive, editorializing weakens them.
- **keep it tight.** twitter rewards density. every sentence should earn its place. if a line doesn't add information or voice, cut it.
- **end with a link, not a call to action.** no "check it out!" or "would love your feedback!" - just drop the link. people know what to do.

## sentence-level patterns

- tends to start with lowercase "i"
- uses dashes for asides and clause breaks, not semicolons or emdashes
- short sentences mixed with occasional longer ones for rhythm
- "so i built/wrote X" is a natural framing - motivation then action
- comfortable saying "i didn't want" or "i like how X does Y but" - honest about preferences without being negative
- qualifies claims when accuracy demands it (parentheticals for benchmark conditions)

## examples of what sounds like him

good:
- "i like how ink does terminal UIs - JSX, flexbox - but i didn't want React and Yoga as dependencies just to render text in a terminal."
- "per-cell diffing, signals, zero dependencies."
- "580x less I/O per render (single-cell update on a 10k cell screen)"

bad:
- "Introducing trend - a revolutionary new TUI framework!"
- "The trick? Per-cell diffing."
- "I'm excited to announce..."
- "Here's why this matters:"

## mental model

write like someone who's been heads-down building for months, finally comes up for air, and tells a friend what they made. no press release energy. no sales pitch. just: here's what i wanted, here's what i built, here's how it performs.
